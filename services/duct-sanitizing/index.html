Duct Sanitizing & Deodorizing Wingham, ON

Sanitizing or deodorizing is a separate choice from removing dust and debris. If a home has an odour associated with smoke, pets, pests, or a period of being closed up, first identify where it is strongest and whether the source may be outside the ductwork. Ask what product is proposed, whether the system must be cleaned first, and what its label says about application and re-entry. A treatment should not be presented as a substitute for finding and correcting an ongoing source.

Questions About the Work

Does an odour return when the system runs?

Note when and where the smell occurs, and check for possible sources outside the ducts. Ask whether cleaning is needed first and request the product label and application instructions before considering a treatment.

  • Describe the Odour and Its Likely Source

    If a home has lingering smoke, pet, or pest odours, describe when they began and which rooms are affected. Odours can originate in furnishings, building cavities, or the ventilation system, so this information affects whether duct treatment is relevant. If there may be an active pest or moisture source, ask that it be assessed separately.

  • Clean Before Considering a Treatment

    If treatment is being considered, ask whether the ducts will first be cleaned and what evidence supports applying a product. Dust can prevent a treatment from reaching the underlying surface, and spraying over debris does not remove that debris. Confirm which parts of the system are included and whether cleaning and treatment are separately priced.

  • Read the Label and Application Directions

    Request the product name and label before agreeing to an application. Check the stated use, application method, ventilation directions, and any re-entry instructions, and ask how these apply to the home. A product should only be used according to its label; no treatment should be described as a health benefit or a guarantee of odour removal.
